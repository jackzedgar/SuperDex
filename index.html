Abstract

Training one policy that tracks thousands of human hand–object demonstrations with a dexterous robot hand is still limited by compute: reinforcement learning with PPO needs many GPU-days, and current generalist trackers are trained on at most a few hundred trajectories. SuperMimic recently showed that, for humanoid whole-body tracking, a differentiable world model fitted to simulator logs lets a policy be optimized by backpropagating through imagined rollouts, reducing training cost by orders of magnitude.

Dexterous manipulation breaks the assumptions behind that result. The object is underactuated and moves only through intermittent contact, every object brings its own geometry and physics, and stick–slip contact makes the dynamics hard to predict. SuperDex brings the differentiable-world-model recipe to dexterous hand–object tracking and, before scaling up, tests the three assumptions most likely to fail in transfer: that object motion is predictable, that analytic gradients through the learned model are useful under contact, and that one model can amortize over many objects.

SuperDex keeps SuperMimic's three-stage loop and changes it in four places: an object-conditioned world model that reads signed-distance features of hand query points, two minimal gradient repairs (pre-contact distance shaping and critic takeover where gradients explode), a capped start distribution, and a perception channel that models pose noise, latency and tracking loss outside the world model. We target a single tracking policy covering all 1,944 OakInk-V2 and HOT3D trajectories (140 objects) at no more than one-fifth of the GPU hours of a same-simulator PPO baseline, evaluated in closed loop on a real xArm7 + Wuji hand.

Why Is This Hard?

SuperMimic reduced the cost of humanoid whole-body tracking from about 21,000 GPU·h to 3 hours on a single H100, and names object interaction as its missing piece. That missing piece is exactly what makes dexterous tracking difficult. Three properties of hand–object interaction put the transfer at risk:

  Underactuated objects

The object moves only through contact. Without contact, $\partial\,\text{object} / \partial\,\text{hand} \approx 0$, so BPTT gradients can vanish during approach and grasp, and become ill-conditioned when contacts switch.

  Object-specific dynamics

All humanoid motions share one body. In dexterous manipulation the hand is shared, but every object has its own geometry, mass and friction.

  Contact sensitivity

Stick–slip and bifurcations with light objects push a deterministic world model toward the mean; object position and orientation errors already dominate the failures of PPO-based trackers.

Method

One Loop, Four Changes

SuperDex keeps SuperMimic's three-stage loop: collect experience in simulation, fit a world model, and improve the policy by backpropagating through the frozen world model. Changes land in exactly four places, each tied to one hypothesis and one diagnostic. Real-robot data flows back into the world model for system identification and residual fine-tuning.

Object-Conditioned World Model

The default world model (W1) adds a single input to SuperMimic's 3×2048 MLP: the local geometry of the object around the hand. For 21 hand query points (5 fingertips and 16 link points), we look up the distance and gradient in a precomputed object signed-distance field (voxels ≤ 3 mm). Because the field is defined everywhere, this feature has a non-zero gradient with respect to the action even before contact happens.

\[ \phi_t=\Big[\max\big(\mathrm{sdf}_o(p_\iota),\,-3\,\mathrm{mm}\big),\ \nabla\mathrm{sdf}_o(p_\iota)\Big]_{\iota=1}^{21}, \qquad p_\iota=\big(T^{o|w}_t\big)^{-1}\,\mathrm{FK}^{w}_\iota(q_t) \]
\[ \Delta\hat s=\mathrm{MLP}_\omega\big([\bar s_t,\ a_t,\ \xi,\ \phi_t]\big),\qquad \hat s_{t+1}=\hat s_t\oplus\Delta\hat s \]
  • Equivariant inputs. States are stored in the world frame and mapped into the wrist's yaw-local frame before entering the network, so a resting object always has a zero predicted increment and the model cannot create "ghost" pulling forces.
  • Simulation context $\xi$. Friction, mass, center of mass, inertia, finger offsets, PD gains and actuation delay are randomized per episode; a fixed 128-d geometry code $b_o$ (BPS-1024 + PCA) identifies the object.
  • Only dynamics is learned. Reward, observation, contact distance and termination are computed analytically from the predicted state. The loss is a normalized $k=8$-step autoregressive state error with object components weighted ×4 and windows sampled with stratification by object.
  • Fallbacks, not defaults. If the decisive tests fail, the object head switches to a contact-wrench model with Newton–Euler integration (W2), or the model gains FiLM conditioning and cross-attention over object points.

Learning Through Imagination, Repaired for Contact

The policy is deterministic and is updated exactly as in SuperMimic: starting from states the simulator actually reached, it imagines $H=32$ steps inside the frozen world model and maximizes the soft-survival return with a critic bootstrap at the horizon:

\[ \hat J(\theta)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Big[\sum_{h=0}^{H-1}\gamma^{h}\,\hat c_{n,h}\,\upsilon_{n,h}\,\hat r_{n,h+1} +\gamma^{H}\,\hat c_{n,H}\,\upsilon_{n,H}\,V_{n,H}\Big] \]

SuperDex adds two minimal repairs, one on the reward side and one on the gradient side.

1 · Pre-contact distance shaping. A reference-gated SDF contact term activates when the reference fingertip is within 1 cm of the surface, and every tracking kernel becomes dual-scale:

\[ \kappa_\sigma(e)=0.7\,e^{-e^{2}/\sigma^{2}}+0.3\,e^{-e^{2}/(5\sigma)^{2}} \]

Gradients then survive up to roughly $10\sigma$ of error. This hardly matters for PPO, but it is critical for BPTT.

2 · Critic takeover where gradients explode. While backpropagating through each imagined state, a sample whose normalized state gradient exceeds $\chi=3$ times the median over active samples has its gradient replaced by a version truncated at that step and bootstrapped by the critic:

\[ G_{n,h}\leftarrow\begin{cases}G^{\mathrm{tr}}_{n,h}, & \|\sigma\odot G_{n,h}\|>\chi\cdot\mathrm{med}_{n'\in\mathcal A_h}\|\sigma\odot G_{n',h}\|\\[2pt] G_{n,h}, & \text{otherwise}\end{cases} \]

Without explosions the update is identical to SuperMimic, and $\chi=\infty$ recovers the original. The rule is a forward-identity, backward-replace autograd hook of about 20 lines. If the decisive test shows the gradient is still unreliable, a simulator-anchored α-mixture of first- and zero-order gradients (a generalization of Suh et al., 2022) is switched on.

DexGradScope — gradient diagnostics stratified by contact phase

free approach contact onset stable contact release slip

To measure whether the gradients are useful, DexGradScope compares the first-order gradient through the world model against a zero-order reference estimated in the true simulator (512 antithetic pairs), with a split-half correction of the cosine. It reports corrected cosine, signal-to-noise ratio, critic-takeover rate and effective smoothing bandwidth $\sigma^{*}$ for each of the five contact phases. It only logs and never steers training.

Capped Start Distribution

Simulator resets are drawn per 0.5 s phase bin, indexed globally across trajectories, from SuperMimic's mixture of 50% uniform, 40% return-based and 10% memory terms, with one addition: no bin may exceed 5× its uniform share.

\[ p_{\mathrm{sim}}(b)=\min\Big(\tfrac{5}{|\mathcal S|},\ \lambda\,\tilde p_b\Big) \]

The cap bounds the budget wasted on unlearnable references without having to identify them first. Resets use physically settled anchor states, and imagination starts from states the simulator actually reached.

Perception Channel Cζ

SuperMimic's additive noise $o=\bar o+\eta$ becomes $o_t=C_\zeta(\bar o_{\le t})$, placed outside the world model:

  • per-episode pose bias and AR(1) jitter that grows with occlusion;
  • a two-state tracked/lost Markov chain whose loss rate rises with occlusion;
  • vision latency sampled from the measured histogram (≈ 50–90 ms) at 30 Hz.

All parameters are fitted on a 2-hour real calibration set with OptiTrack ground truth. Because the world model predicts only noise-free states, changing the perception setup does not require retraining it.

Three Hypotheses, Three Decisive Tests

Rather than building the full system first, SuperDex turns each risk of the transfer into a pre-registered, falsifiable test on Probe-40, a set of 40 trajectories in four strata (stable grasp-and-carry, in-hand reorientation or regrasp, bimanual handover, and light-object tool use). Each test has a pre-written fallback, and a failed test is itself a result: it tells us where analytic gradients through learned models stop working under dexterous contact.

A1Predictable

Over 8 steps, object-motion prediction error stays far below the success threshold (3 cm / 30°), including at contact switches.

A2Useful gradients

BPTT through the world model reaches PPO's success rate with far less compute, including before contact and at contact switches.

A3Amortizable

Growing from 10 to 120 objects barely raises world-model error, and error on held-out objects stays bounded.

DDeployable

Simulated success carries over to the real hand in closed loop, measured as a sim-to-real retention ratio.

Experimental Design

Every claim made by DexMimic gets a matching SuperDex number under the same protocol, plus experiments it does not have: world-model fidelity, gradient quality and quantified closed-loop deployment. Main conclusions come only from controlled comparisons with the same simulator, hardware, observations and rewards, reported in both GPU·h and environment steps.

Data and Splits

OakInk-V2 + HOT3D provide 1,944 trajectories (1,302 single-hand, 642 bimanual) over 140 objects, retargeted to the Wuji hand in MuJoCo and resampled to 50 Hz. A physical-feasibility filter is applied with per-stage counts published, and all splits are frozen and hashed before any training.

Metrics

  • Tracking: success rate with DexMimic thresholds (30° / 3 cm / 8 cm / 6 cm), a strict variant (15° / 1.5 cm), per-quantity errors, and CVaR10 — the mean success of the worst 10% of trajectories.
  • Efficiency: H100 GPU·h to target success (including data collection and world-model training), simulator steps and imagined steps.
  • World model: 8- and 32-step object error, stratified by contact phase and object.
  • Gradients: DexGradScope's per-phase metrics.

Statistical Protocol

  • 5 seeds for main claims, 3 seeds elsewhere; diverged runs are counted and reported.
  • 50 rollouts per trajectory with randomized $\xi$; final success is measured only in the simulator, never from imagined returns.
  • 95% confidence intervals from a hierarchical bootstrap (seeds, then trajectories); paired comparisons per trajectory; Holm correction for ablations.
  • Thresholds, splits and the two primary metrics (success rate and CVaR10) are pre-registered.

Real-World Deployment

SuperDex is evaluated in closed loop with quantified trial statistics, using the same 8 skills and 8 objects as DexMimic's real-robot experiments and pairing every condition with its open-loop replay protocol.

Hardware and Perception

  • Robot: xArm7 arm with a 20-DoF Wuji right hand.
  • Perception: two fixed RealSense D435i cameras; FoundationPose tracks the object pose at 30 Hz. OptiTrack provides ground truth for evaluation and calibration only.
  • Control: 50 Hz real-time C++ loop, arm setpoints interpolated to 250 Hz, a 50 ms watchdog and velocity, torque and workspace limits.
  • Objects: the same 8 3D-printed OakInk objects, weighed to within 5% of the nominal mass.
  • System identification: step and chirp excitation for the arm (wrist step error < 3 mm), fingers and fingertip contact softness.

Real Data Flows Back into the World Model

  ξ inference

With the world model frozen, infer the hand parameters and per-object physics from 8-step real windows, then narrow the simulator's randomization around the estimate and keep training.

  Residual fine-tuning

A zero-initialized 2×256 residual on top of the frozen model, trained only on real windows and used only for imagination that starts from real states.

  Latency compensation

Optionally roll the world model forward from the last valid visual pose using proprioception and issued commands to estimate the current object pose.

Results are reported with Wilson 95% intervals and paired McNemar tests, together with the sim-to-real retention ratio (real success / simulated success). Comparing OptiTrack and FoundationPose perception separates the perception gap from the dynamics gap.

Expected Contributions

  1. The first generalist dexterous hand–object tracker trained with a learned differentiable world model and BPTT, compared against re-implemented DexMimic and PPO in the same simulator, on the same hardware and over multiple seeds, targeting ≤ 1/5 of their GPU·h.
  2. An object-conditioned world model with an error curve showing whether one model amortizes over a hundred objects, or where it stops.
  3. A contact-phase analysis of learned-model gradients under dexterous contact — DexGradScope, first- vs. zero-order gradient maps and effective smoothing bandwidth — together with two minimal repairs.
  4. Quantified closed-loop real-robot evaluation: 8 skills, 520 trials, paired against open-loop replay, with a calibrated perception channel and real data fed back into the world model.
  5. A rigorous, open evaluation protocol: three evaluation sets, pre-registered thresholds and a same-simulator re-implementation of DexMimic.
